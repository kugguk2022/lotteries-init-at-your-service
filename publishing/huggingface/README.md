# LottoBench Community Benchmark

A deterministic, redistribution-safe **software** benchmark for comparing lottery strategy providers
under forward-only, equal-budget evaluation. It measures implementation behaviour — portfolio
coverage, diversity, crowding, search-space reduction, and reproducibility — on synthetic draws. It
is not evidence that a fair lottery is predictable, and it contains no operator-sourced data.

- **Leaderboard Space:** [kugguk/lottobench-community-leaderboard](https://huggingface.co/spaces/kugguk/lottobench-community-leaderboard)
- **Source code:** [kugguk2022/lotteries-init-at-your-service](https://github.com/kugguk2022/lotteries-init-at-your-service)
- **Benchmark version:** `3.0.0` · **LottoBench:** `0.1.0a4` · **seed:** `20260828`
- **Snapshot digest:** `a7ab8eb08182a09d8d33507e42a6d1b98ec830d5e2db0d13e0a177dd86938c47`

> **Version 3.0.0 supersedes 2.0.0 and its numbers are not comparable.** 2.0.0 scored POI-G
> containment only on the whole ticket, over a 4-draw holdout. Both were wrong: the holdout could
> not resolve containment at all, and full-ticket scoring charges a main-pool reducer for an axis it
> barely ranks on. 3.0.0 uses a 260-draw history, scores containment on both axes against their own
> baselines, and publishes the auxiliary-axis degeneracy that caused the artifact. See
> [Two containment axes](#two-containment-axes).

## What this benchmark does and does not show

| Question | Answer from this dataset |
|---|---|
| Does coordinating several providers beat the best single provider at equal budget? | Yes on the primary metric — coordinated aggregation reaches **0.4050** pair coverage vs **0.3814** for the best single provider (**+6.2 %**). |
| Does any provider predict the next draw? | No. `hit_recall` lands at 0.336–0.349 for every provider against a fair-draw expectation of `k/n` = **0.3333**. That is noise around the null. |
| Does POI-G reduce the search space usefully? | Unresolved here. Containment lift sits at **0.48 / 1.07 / 1.07** (main axis) — indistinguishable from 1.0. See the caveat below: this data *cannot* show a lift. |
| Can the extensive POI-G subset be inspected directly? | Yes. `poi_g_candidates` publishes all 500 ranked candidates; `poi_g_fixed_budget` contains only the five tickets eligible for modeled or realized ROI. |
| Does any strategy produce positive ROI? | No. Modelled jackpot-tier ROI is between **−0.92 and −0.95** per ticket everywhere. |
| Is the run reproducible? | Yes. Fixed seed, frozen game shape, and a SHA-256 over the draw snapshot pinned in the manifest. |

> **The decisive caveat for the POI-G track.** `synthetic_history.csv` is generated by
> `numpy.random.default_rng(20260828).choice` — it is uniform by construction, with no
> co-occurrence structure to find. **No method can exceed containment lift 1.0 on it, in
> expectation.** A lift near 1.0 here confirms the harness is calibrated; it is *not* evidence for
> or against POI-G. Any claim about POI-G on real draws needs a non-uniform or real-data track,
> which this dataset does not provide.

## Results at a glance

### Provider track — primary metric `pair_coverage`

52-draw holdout, 5 tickets per provider per draw.

| provider | hit_recall | pair_coverage | number_coverage | mean_jaccard_diversity | expected_roi_per_ticket | unpopularity_lift |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| **coordinated_aggregation** | 0.3471 | **0.4050** | 0.9215 | 0.8140 | −0.9424 | 1.1598 |
| uniform_random | 0.3413 | 0.3814 | 0.8766 | 0.7853 | −0.9463 | 1.0815 |
| frequency | 0.3365 | 0.3776 | 0.8622 | 0.7747 | −0.9485 | 1.0358 |
| unpopularity | 0.3490 | 0.2421 | 0.5849 | 0.5276 | **−0.9218** | **1.5733** |

Coordinated aggregation wins the primary metric, which is the design claim under test: recombining
already-proposed tickets under a shared budget reaches more of the combinatorial space than any
contributing provider spending the same five tickets. The `unpopularity` provider deliberately
sacrifices coverage to capture the shared-jackpot lever, which gives it the highest
`unpopularity_lift` and the least-negative ROI — still a losing number.

`hit_recall` is spread across 0.336–0.349 with a fair-draw expectation of 4/12 = 0.3333. Every
provider sits on the null. That is the correct result on uniform data, and it is why `hit_recall`
is a secondary metric here rather than a headline.

### POI-G track — primary metric `containment_lift_main`

104-draw holdout. Universe: 1980 full tickets, 495 main-pool combinations.

| shortlist | distinct main sets | main contained | main baseline | **main lift** | full contained | full baseline | full lift | star entropy |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 20 | 19.9 | 2 / 104 (1.92 %) | 4.01 % | **0.48** | 0 / 104 (0.00 %) | 1.01 % | 0.00 | 0.74 |
| 100 | 93.7 | 21 / 104 (20.19 %) | 18.93 % | **1.07** | 7 / 104 (6.73 %) | 5.05 % | 1.33 | 0.96 |
| 500 | 345.7 | 78 / 104 (75.00 %) | 69.83 % | **1.07** | 25 / 104 (24.04 %) | 25.25 % | 0.95 | 0.97 |

Every lift is within sampling noise of 1.0 on data that cannot contain a signal. Read this as a
calibration check that passed, not as a verdict on the method.

## Two containment axes

POI-G ranks legal tickets by distance from a causal pair-co-occurrence target `G`. That target is
built almost entirely from main-pool pair counts, and ties in the integer `G` score are broken by
enumeration order, which iterates star combinations in the outer loop. The consequence is
measurable and is published as `star_share_entropy` and `min_star_share`:

- At shortlist size **20**, the least-represented auxiliary number takes **1.83 %** of shortlist
  slots against a uniform 25 %, and star entropy is **0.74**. The shortlist holds ~19.9 *distinct*
  main combinations in 20 slots — it is spending its entire budget on the main axis.
- By size **100** the degeneracy largely clears (entropy 0.96, min share 16.9 %).

So at small sizes the shortlist is effectively a **main-combination shortlist**, and scoring it on
full-ticket containment measures the auxiliary degeneracy rather than the reducer. That is exactly
what produced the misleading `0.00` at size 20 — the size-20 shortlist *did* contain the correct
main combination on 2 of 104 draws, each time paired with a star it had not ranked.

Both axes are published because each answers a different question:

| metric | denominator | answers |
|---|---|---|
| `containment_lift_main` | `mean_distinct_main_sets / C(12,4)` | Does the reducer find the right *numbers*? |
| `containment_lift_full` | `subset_size / 1980` | Would you actually hold the winning *ticket*? |

Using the full-ticket denominator to score a main-only hit compares against the wrong baseline and
will understate any main-pool reducer. Report the pair, not one of them.

## Files and schema

### `data/synthetic_history.csv` — config `history`, 260 rows

Five years of weekly synthetic draws for a 4-of-12 main pool plus a 1-of-4 auxiliary pool, generated
from `numpy.random.default_rng(20260828)`. No operator data of any kind.

| column | type | meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `draw_date` | string (ISO date) | Synthetic weekly draw date, starting 2025-01-01. |
| `ball_1` … `ball_4` | int | Main-pool numbers, ascending, drawn without replacement from 1–12. |
| `star_1` | int | Auxiliary-pool number from 1–4. |

### `data/benchmark_results.csv` — config `results`, 4 rows

One row per provider plus one for the coordinated aggregator, scored on the final 52 draws.

| column | type | meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `benchmark_version`, `dataset_sha256` | string | Comparability keys — rows are only comparable when both match. |
| `provider` | string | `uniform_random`, `frequency`, `unpopularity`, or `coordinated_aggregation`. |
| `budget`, `holdout`, `seed` | int | Tickets per draw (5), scored draws (52), RNG seed. |
| `hit_recall` | float 0–1 | Mean fraction of a ticket's main numbers appearing in the actual draw. Fair-draw expectation is `main_k / main_n`. |
| `pair_coverage` | float 0–1 | **Primary.** Fraction of all C(12,2) = 66 number pairs covered by at least one ticket. |
| `number_coverage` | float 0–1 | Fraction of the 12-number main pool touched by the portfolio. |
| `mean_jaccard_diversity` | float 0–1 | 1 − mean pairwise Jaccard similarity; 1 = fully disjoint tickets. |
| `expected_roi_per_ticket` | float | Modelled jackpot-tier `E[payout] · P(win) / price − 1`. Always negative. |
| `unpopularity_lift` | float | Portfolio expected payout ÷ that of an equally sized uniformly popular portfolio. Above 1 means the portfolio leans unpopular. |

### `data/poi_g_subset_results.csv` — config `poi_g_subsets`, 3 rows

POI-G is a **search-space reducer**, not a five-ticket provider. It ranks legal tickets by distance
from the next causal pair-co-occurrence target, fitted on a 26-draw trailing window of strictly
earlier rows. Containment is scored on the whole shortlist; ROI is scored only on the five
top-ranked tickets, because those are the only ones anyone would actually buy.

| column | type | meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `method`, `subset_size`, `holdout` | string, int, int | `poi_g_causal`, the requested shortlist size, and the 104 scored draws. |
| `universe_size`, `universe_fraction`, `reduction_factor` | int, float, float | 1980 legal tickets, the shortlist's share of them, and `universe_size / subset_size`. |
| `contained_draws_full`, `containment_rate_full` | int, float | Draws whose **complete ticket** (mains + star) fell inside the shortlist. |
| `random_expected_containment_rate_full` | float | `subset_size / 1980`. |
| `containment_lift_full` | float | Full-ticket rate ÷ its baseline. 1.0 = no better than random. |
| `main_universe_size`, `mean_distinct_main_sets` | int, float | C(12,4) = 495, and how many distinct main combinations the shortlist actually held. |
| `contained_draws_main`, `containment_rate_main` | int, float | Draws whose **main combination** fell inside the shortlist, star ignored. |
| `random_expected_containment_rate_main` | float | `mean_distinct_main_sets / 495` — the main-only baseline, not the full-ticket one. |
| `containment_lift_main` | float | **Primary.** Main-only rate ÷ its own baseline. |
| `star_share_entropy` | float 0–1 | How evenly shortlist slots spread over the auxiliary pool. 1.0 = uniform; low values mean full-ticket containment understates the reducer. |
| `min_star_share` | float | Share of slots taken by the least-represented auxiliary number. Uniform would be 0.25. |
| `selection_budget` | int | Tickets taken off the top of the shortlist for the ROI column (5). |
| `modeled_expected_roi_per_selected_ticket` | float | Modelled ROI of *those five tickets only* — never of buying the shortlist. |

### `data/benchmark_manifest.json`

Frozen game shape, seed, budget, holdouts, provider list, metric names, snapshot digest, and the
claims boundary. Load this first when comparing runs.

### POI-G candidate snapshot — configs `poi_g_candidates` and `poi_g_fixed_budget`

`poi_g_candidates` exposes the complete 500-row ranked search-space reduction for the next
synthetic snapshot. Every row carries its draw key, training-history hash, G score, distance from
the causal target, crowd-popularity share, modeled expected ROI, and an integrity hash.

`poi_g_fixed_budget` contains exactly the first five ranked candidates. Only these five appear in
`data/poi_g_prediction.json`, the sealed inference envelope eligible for settlement. The extensive
subset is never priced as though all 500 candidates were purchased. File hashes and the modeled
portfolio ROI are recorded in `data/poi_g_candidate_manifest.json`.

## Evaluation protocol

- **Forward-only.** At each holdout step `t`, every provider and every POI-G shortlist is fitted on
  `history[:t]` alone. No row at or after `t` is visible during fitting, so there is no leakage path.
- **Equal budget.** Every provider — and the aggregator — proposes exactly 5 tickets per step.
  Coverage comparisons are meaningless at unequal budgets.
- **Holdouts.** 52 draws for the provider track, 104 for POI-G containment. Containment is a rare
  binary event and needs the longer window; 2.0.0's 4-draw holdout could not resolve it at all.
- **Frozen game.** `main_n=12, main_k=4, auxiliary_n=4, auxiliary_k=1`, giving 1980 legal tickets
  over 495 main combinations.
- **Deterministic.** Seed `20260828` throughout; the aggregator is a greedy submodular selection
  that is deterministic given identical envelopes.
- **ROI model.** Jackpot 1000, ticket price 2.0, 10 000 other tickets. Jackpot tier only — lower
  prize tiers are not modelled, so ROI here is a comparative diagnostic, not a payout forecast.

changed and your numbers are not comparable to the ones above.

## Submitting results

Results may be compared under the same `benchmark_version` **only** when the snapshot digest,
provider set, budget, holdout, seed, and scoring code are all unchanged. Change any of them and bump
the version instead — a leaderboard whose rows were produced under different conditions is worse
than no leaderboard. Open an issue or discussion on the
[source repository](https://github.com/kugguk2022/lotteries-init-at-your-service/issues) to submit.

## Limitations and responsible use

- **The synthetic draws are uniform by construction.** They can validate a harness; they cannot
  support or refute a claim that any method finds structure in real draws. The POI-G track in
  particular is a calibration check here, not a test of the method.
- The draws are synthetic. Nothing here transfers to an operated lottery, and no result should be
  quoted as if it did.
- Containment is a rare binary event. Even at a 104-draw holdout the lift columns carry wide
  intervals; treat differences under roughly 1.5x as unresolved.
- `expected_roi_per_ticket` and `modeled_expected_roi_per_selected_ticket` are **modelled**
  quantities from a single-tier jackpot model, not realised returns.
- LottoBench does not place wagers, recommend gambling, improve mechanical odds, or give financial
  advice. The safest financial baseline is not to play. If gambling is causing harm, contact a local
  support service such as [BeGambleAware](https://www.begambleaware.org/).
- The MIT license covers these synthetic artifacts and the LottoBench code. It grants no rights to
  third-party lottery data, names, marks, or services.
